What the app can honestly know

The sand cannot show what the API will not say

Screen Time hands Furlough exact window times and exact statuses, but never a usage number. Budget is known only at the thresholds it fires. Every direction below is built on this table.

Direction A

Window glass

The sand is window time. The top bulb holds what is left of the window and empties exactly at close, in step with the countdown. Budget lives beside it: the sub line, and an amber tint on the glass after the 5-minute warning.
Costs nothing new. Everything it shows is exact.
Trusts only the clock.
Does not show usage. On a 2-hour window with a 30-minute budget the sand drains for two hours whether you touch the app or not.
Direction B

Budget glass

The sand is budget, in quarters. The level steps down a quarter at each threshold callback and settles; after the 5-minute warning the last grains run under a faster amber pulse; spent means every grain at the bottom, ember, still. This is the "half an hour left" reading.
Costs three more threshold events per target, a new event name, a day-keyed quarter map, and a monitor change.
Trusts the monitor's timing: steps can land minutes late.
The stream runs while the window is open, not while you are using the app. The API cannot tell the two apart, so the stream is a mood, not a measurement.
Direction C

Hybrid

Top bulb is the window, exact and draining, from A. The bottom mound's colour carries the budget: sand while plenty is left, amber after the 5-minute warning, ember once spent. Optional quarter marks on the base if the extra thresholds go in.
Costs nothing without marks; with marks, the same three events per target as B.
Trusts the clock for the drain and the callbacks only for colour, which they get right even when late.
Shows both facts in one object, each at the precision the API allows.

Header layouts

Three ways to page through the apps

Six managed apps in the list's own order: Anchored, Open now, Later today, Tomorrow, Always blocked, Needs a schedule. The phones are live: swipe the pager, tap the strip, tap a bar in the deck. The list below the hero stays in every layout.

H1 · Horizontal paging (the brief's recommendation). One page per app, swiped like Weather. The page indicator is a row of tiny hourglasses in status colours, so the strip itself is a status summary. Tapping a page opens that app's rule editor. Lands on the first open app.
H2 · Thumbnail strip. One big hero plus a strip of small coloured hourglasses, one per app; tapping one swaps the hero with a matched-geometry move. Denser than paging, so it holds up better with many apps.
H3 · Vertical deck. The hero is a stack: the front card is expanded and the others peek below it as thin status bars; drag or tap to bring the next forward. The most dramatic, and the hardest to make feel native inside the main scroll view.
If it were mine

What I would build

C with quarter marks, paged as H1

  • The drain is real. The top bulb tracks the countdown to the second, so the glass and the numbers never disagree, and the animation is continuous rather than four jumps a day.
  • The budget is still in the glass. The mound turns amber at the warning and ember when spent, which are the two callbacks that matter, and the base marks add the quarters once the extra thresholds prove reliable on your phone.
  • Nothing pretends. B's running stream would imply usage the API cannot see. C keeps the stream tied to the window, which is what it actually measures.
  • H1 reads at a glance. The indicator strip doubles as the status summary you asked for, and paging matches how iOS already teaches people to move between things.
